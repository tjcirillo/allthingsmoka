How My Moka Journey Began

I did not begin with a grand plan. I began with a moka pot, curiosity, and the suspicion that this simple brewer could make better coffee than I was getting from it.

That curiosity became a long series of experiments: different pots, different coffees, different grinders, heat levels, timing, yields, and stopping points.

What changed

The biggest lesson was that moka brewing is not mysterious—but it is sensitive. Small changes in grind, heat, and when you stop the brew can noticeably change the cup.

The goal is not ritual for its own sake. The goal is repeatable coffee you actually enjoy.

I also learned that many confident moka rules are not universal. Pot size, burner, coffee, grinder, and personal taste all matter.

What I would advise now

Start simply. Use a medium-fine grind, moderate heat, and stop before the final violent sputtering. Change one variable at a time. Keep the changes that improve the cup and ignore the ones that merely make the process more complicated.
